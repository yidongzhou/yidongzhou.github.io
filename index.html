Yidong Zhou

Assistant Professor in Statistics at the University of Minnesota

I work on machine learning, causal inference, and nonparametric statistics, with a particular interest in data such as distributions, networks, and curves.

Publications Google Scholar
ydzhou@umn.edu ORCID GitHub LinkedIn

I received my Ph.D. in Statistics from UC Davis in 2024, advised by Professor Hans-Georg Müller, and my B.S. in Statistics from the University of Science and Technology of China in 2019.

Before joining Minnesota, I was a postdoctoral scholar at UC Davis. I was also a research intern at Amazon in 2022.

Students and collaborators

If you are interested in working with me, please email me. I am happy to discuss both methodological questions and applications involving complex or longitudinal data.

Research

Much of my work uses the geometry of data to guide statistical modeling and inference. I study probability distributions, networks, functions, compositions, and positive-definite matrices, with applications in neuroimaging, child development, economics and policy evaluation, and plant genomics.

Machine learning

Neural networks, tree-based methods, and transfer learning for complex outcomes.

Causal inference

Causal effects with structured outcomes, with a focus on difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, and synthetic control.

Nonparametric statistics

Fréchet regression and inference for distributions, networks, functions, and manifold-valued data.

Functional data analysis

Longitudinal trajectories and sparsely observed functional data, motivated by biomedical and population studies.
